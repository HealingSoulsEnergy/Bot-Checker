    Before you do anything

    Hang up and call back on a number you already know, not one the caller gives you. If it's about a family member, call that person directly, or another relative who would know.

    A familiar voice proves nothing now. A few seconds of someone's voice from a video online is enough to clone it. Many families agree on a safe word that only they know, and ask for it when a call feels wrong.

    Report it: reportfraud.ftc.gov. If you already sent money or gift card numbers, call your bank or the gift card company right away.

    Check it before you share it

    This app can spot manipulation tricks, but it can't tell you whether a claim is true. The fastest check is to see if other real outlets are reporting the same thing. If only one strange site has it, be very doubtful.

    Search Google News Search fact checks Search the web

    For a video or photo: fake videos are now good enough that how they look proves very little. Check whether the person in it posted it on their own verified account, or whether major outlets are covering it. To trace a photo, use Google Lens or TinEye to see where else it appeared and when.

    Real people can be wrong or misleading too, not just AI fakes. A genuine recording of a real person still needs its claims checked. Look at what is being claimed, not only who is saying it.

    For a speech or someone talking: AI can now copy a person's voice closely, and no app, including this one, can reliably tell a cloned voice by listening. Find the full original from the speaker's own channel, C-SPAN, or a major outlet, and compare. Short clips are often cut to change what was meant, and a real recording can still be taken out of context. YouTube and most video sites have a transcript you can copy into the box above.

    Check a loan or credit offer

    Call your lender or card company using the number on your statement or the back of your card, never the number in the offer. Ask them directly whether they sent it.

    Free mortgage help: HUD-approved housing counselors, 1-800-569-4287. There is no fee.

    Stop prescreened credit offers: 1-888-567-8688 (1-888-5-OPT-OUT) or optoutprescreen.com, run by the credit bureaus.

    Check it with the real source

    Don't use the phone number, website, or return envelope in the letter. Look up the company or agency yourself and call the number you find. A car warranty question goes to your dealer, and a business filing goes straight to the Ohio Secretary of State at ohiosos.gov.

    Report mail fraud: U.S. Postal Inspection Service, 1-877-876-2455, or uspis.gov/report

    Check it with the real source

    Don't call the number in the ad. If you want to know what you really qualify for, call these free, unbiased lines yourself. Nobody there is selling a plan.

    Medicare: 1-800-633-4227 (1-800-MEDICARE), open 24 hours, or medicare.gov/plan-compare

    Ohio OSHIIP (free Medicare counselors from the Ohio Department of Insurance): 1-800-686-1578

    Medicare's open enrollment runs October 15 to December 7, which is when these ads are thickest.

    Check the site with outside tools

    This app can't open the website itself. These free tools can, and they open in a new tab. A site that is only a few weeks or months old and sells name brands cheap is almost always fake.
